My Experiment 4 was a map-making game called Kitbashing that I don’t think was altogether successful, but it was, at least, an interesting concept. I just ran out of time to write this week (an unfortunate side effect of being a student in November), and so it was hard to get a full idea of what the game would actually be like. In addition, part of the catch of the game is coming up with your own theme/genre, which is interesting when you combine them, but it also makes the game itself feel much less specifically authored. It reads more like an SRD or a world-building tool than an actual game.

I was more interested in Michael’s game, which was a magic battler based on drawing out actual, physical spells. I wish I had taken a more mechanics-focused route for this week’s experiment (or, honestly, any of them), because the intricacies of how to balance a magic system and make it fun and engaging was really fun to talk about during playtesting.

I think I’ve come to a conclusion at this point, but I was a little unsure of what experiment to select for my final project. It was mostly between Experiments 1 and 2, ADS and Blood Spores, ADS being a solo journaling game and Blood Spores being a more traditional dungeon-crawling TTRPG. While I’m still definitely intrigued to see where Blood Spores would have gone (and I might finish it at some point), I just feel much more interested in ADS conceptually and aesthetically. Blood Spores has more obvious points of expansion, as it wasn’t really a complete game by any means when I playtested it and in fact we honed in on a much more refined version of the game in discussing it. However, ADS is just something that appeals to me more and also, I think, fits in better with my general direction as a designer (which I will flesh out in an artist’s statement shortly after writing this reflection). Now that I’m fleshing out the points of each one in this reflection, I’m still a little unsure of my decision, but I can always come back to Blood Spores after the fact.

As for what to do with ADS, I think I mostly want to mechanize it a bit more. I’d like to tinker with what systems feel best; since the game is so inspired by Death Stranding, I can certainly start by adapting things from those games, but a lot of that probably won’t work very well in a singleplayer journaling TTRPG context. I could bake a little extra worldbuilding into the game setup, although I do really like the emergent worldbuilding that you get from playing it and being forced to write about something. I think the biggest lever I have to pull in terms of making gameplay feel different is the amount of time a delivery takes. Because the idea is to keep writing for the entire length of the delivery, but not any longer, it builds a sort of creative tension as you’re writing, and you really feel the pressure to finish your thoughts before the time runs out. In the first playtest, I had that determined by the length of whatever song you were listening to, but maybe there could me more variables to that - difficult terrain, saboteurs, walking slower with a heavy delivery, etc. I could also build out a whole stat system to make traversal more interesting, but that seems like the kind of thing that would not translate as well from the source material.

Bonus thought: is it against the spirit of the final project to go with something so similar to an existing game? I’ll try to make it as unique as possible, but should it be something entirely original instead? Is there such thing as something being entirely original?
